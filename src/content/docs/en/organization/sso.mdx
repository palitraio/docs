Single sign-on (SSO) lets employees sign in to Palitra through your company identity provider
(IdP) — for example Okta, Azure AD, Google Workspace or Keycloak. SAML 2.0 and OpenID Connect
(OIDC) are supported. An organization can have one SSO connection.

Only the organization's owner sees and configures the "Single Sign-On" tab. Admins don't see
it.

Single sign-on is part of the Enterprise plan. If your plan offers the "SSO / SAML" add-on,
you can add it. Without either, instead of the settings you see "SSO / SAML isn't included in your
plan" with the buttons "Buy add-on" and "Upgrade plan". Both lead to the "Billing" tab — see
[Billing](/organization/billing/).

## Open the tab

1. Click your name at the bottom of the sidebar and choose "Organization settings".
2. Go to the "Single Sign-On" tab.

Until a connection exists, the tab reads "Sign in with your company identity provider. Supports
SAML 2.0 and OIDC." and has a "Set up SSO" button.

## Set up SSO

Click "Set up SSO" to open the six-step "Set Up Single Sign-On" wizard. The step number is shown
at the top, for example "Step 1 of 6". Move forward with "Continue" and back with "Back". The
wizard won't move on until the step's required fields are filled in.

Keep your IdP's admin console at hand: some values come from it, and some go into it from
Palitra.

<Screenshot
  id="organization-sso-wizard"
  alt="The Set Up Single Sign-On wizard at the protocol step"
/>

### Step 1. Protocol

1. Choose the "Protocol": "SAML 2.0" or "OpenID Connect (OIDC)".
2. Enter the "Connection name", for example `Acme Corp SSO` — up to 100 characters. The name is
   shown in Palitra, for example in the invite dialog on the "Members" tab.

The protocol can't be changed later: when you edit the connection, the wizard starts at step 2.
To switch protocols, delete the connection and create a new one.

### Step 2. Provider details

Fill in the fields from your application's settings in the IdP.

For SAML 2.0:

- "IdP Entity ID"
- "IdP SSO URL"
- "X.509 Certificate" — the IdP's signing certificate.
- "Name ID Format" — optional, for example
  `urn:oasis:names:tc:SAML:1.1:nameid-format:emailAddress`.

For OIDC:

- "Issuer URL"
- "Client ID"
- "Client Secret" — "Stored encrypted at rest. Never exposed in API responses."

### Step 3. Email domains

In "Allowed email domains", add your company's email domains, for example `acme.com`. Type a
domain and press Enter or a comma; you can add several. At least one is required.

Palitra uses these domains to decide who enforced SSO applies to (see
[Enforced SSO](#enforced-sso)).

A domain can belong to only one organization in Palitra. Errors at this step:

- "Invalid domain. Use the format: example.com" — the domain has a typo, for example a leading
  `@`.
- "This domain is already claimed by another organization." — the domain is already listed in
  another organization's SSO connection.

### Step 4. Roles

This step sets the organization role a person gets when signing in through SSO.

- "Default role" — "Owner", "Admin" or "Member". The default is "Member".
- "Role claim attribute" — optional. The name of the attribute (claim) in the IdP's response
  that determines the role, for example `groups`.
- "Role mapping" — a "Claim value" → "Role" table. Add rows with "Add mapping", remove them
  with "Remove row".

If the user's claim value matches a table row, they get that row's role; otherwise they get the
default role. When a role claim is set, the role is recalculated on every SSO sign-in: a role
you changed by hand on the "Members" tab is overwritten at the next sign-in.

### Step 5. Settings

- "Enable SSO" — on by default. Turn it off to stop SSO without deleting the configuration.
- "Enforce SSO" — off by default. When on, members have to sign in through SSO. Owners and
  members with SSO bypass can still sign in with a password.

### Step 6. Review

The wizard shows everything you entered under "General", "Roles", "Settings" and "Service
Provider Metadata". Palitra's metadata (Entity ID, metadata file) appears after saving: it is
marked "Available after saving.".

Click "Save". Once saved, the tab shows the connection card.

### If saving fails

- "One or more email domains are already claimed by another organization." — the wizard goes
  back to step 3 and marks the taken domains. Remove them and save again.
- "The IdP configuration is invalid. Please check your settings and try again." — the wizard
  goes back to step 2. Compare the values with your IdP console.
- "Something went wrong. Please try again." — try saving again later.

## Give Palitra's details to the IdP

For the IdP to accept sign-ins to Palitra, it needs Palitra's addresses. They are on the
connection card, under "Service provider metadata":

- for SAML — the "ACS URL" and the metadata file: click "Download metadata" and import the file
  into the IdP;
- for OIDC — the "Redirect URI": add it to the application's allowed redirect URIs in the IdP.

## The connection card

After setup the tab shows a card with the connection name, the protocol ("SAML" or "OIDC") and
an "Active" or "Inactive" badge. The card has:

- "Login URL" — an address like `https://…/orgs/<slug>/login`, with "Copy" and "Open in new
  tab" buttons. "Share this URL with your team so they can sign in via SSO."
- "Email domains" — the domains from step 3.
- "Default role", "Role claim" and "Role mapping" — the settings from step 4.
- "Access control" — the "Enabled" and "Enforced" switches. They change the setting at once,
  without the wizard. If that fails: "Failed to update the setting. Please try again.".
- "Service provider metadata" — the details for the IdP (see above).
- "Diagnostics" — a connection check.

The slug in the login URL is the organization's "URL slug" from the
[General](/organization/general/) tab. If you change it, the login URL changes too.

## Check the connection

1. On the card, under "Diagnostics", click "Run diagnostic".
2. If everything is fine, you see "Config is reachable".
3. If not, you see a "Diagnostic errors" list. Fix the settings with "Edit".

## How members sign in

Share the login URL with your team. A person opens it, signs in at your IdP and lands in
Palitra.

On the first SSO sign-in Palitra creates the account if it doesn't exist yet and adds the person
to the organization with a role from the step 4 rules. You don't need to invite them
separately.

If you invite people by hand, the invite dialog on the "Members" tab reminds you: "This
organization uses SSO (…)". See [Members](/organization/members/).

## Enforced SSO

With "Enforced" on, an organization member whose email is on one of the connection's domains
can't sign in with a password — only through SSO. The exceptions:

- the organization's owner can always sign in with a password, so access isn't lost if the IdP
  is down;
- members with SSO bypass. The owner grants it on the "Members" tab: "Grant SSO bypass" in the
  member's menu. See [Members](/organization/members/#sso-bypass).

Before turning enforcement on, run the diagnostic and make sure SSO sign-in works.

## Edit the connection

1. On the card, click "Edit".
2. The "Edit SSO Configuration" wizard opens at step 2: the protocol can't be changed.
3. Enter the IdP details again: "Re-enter your IdP credentials below. Secrets are not returned by
   the server and must be provided again."
4. Go through the steps and click "Save".

## Delete the connection

1. On the card, click "Delete connection".
2. Confirm in the "Delete SSO connection?" dialog with "Delete".

SSO is turned off for the organization. People who signed in only through SSO lose access
unless they have SSO bypass. If deletion fails: "Failed to delete the SSO connection. Please try
again.".

## Loading errors

"Failed to load SSO configuration. Please try again." — reload the page.
